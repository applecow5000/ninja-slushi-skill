# Additives & Texture (Xanthan, Guar, Allulose & Co.)

Distilled from the r/ninjaslushi community dump (not the official manual). This
is the community's most-requested technique area: how to fix icy/watery texture,
replicate a 7-Eleven **Slurpee** (not an ICEE), and make **sugar-free/diet**
drinks actually slush. Amounts are community-reported starting points — mix small
and adjust.

## The key distinction: sweeteners vs. binders

Two different jobs, and confusing them is the #1 mistake:

- **Sweeteners / freezing agents** (sugar, allulose, etc.) — these lower the
  freezing point so the drink forms *fine* slush instead of a solid block. This is
  what the machine's sugar sensor requires. **A binder cannot do this job.**
- **Texture binders** (xanthan gum, guar gum) — these thicken and hold the slush
  together but do **NOT** help it freeze. Used *alone* on a sugar-free drink they
  fail; used *with* a proper sweetener they upgrade the texture.

> If a diet/sugar-free drink comes out as hard ice or won't slush, you have a
> **sweetener** problem, not a binder problem. Add allulose first; add xanthan
> only to fine-tune texture.

## Xanthan gum (and guar gum)

**What it does (community-confirmed):**
- Turns coarse, icy, snow-cone slush into a **fine, cohesive Slurpee-like**
  texture — repeatedly called a "game changer."
- **Stops separation:** without it, the syrup drinks out first and you're left
  with flavorless ice at the bottom (like a melted gas-station slush). Xanthan
  keeps flavor bound to the ice.

**How to use it (this matters):**
- **Dose: ~¼ teaspoon per batch** of soda or juice is the common starting point.
  Some go up to ~1 tsp for a full 64 oz — start low.
- **Dissolve/mix it in BEFORE pouring.** Do not dump dry gum into the vessel.
- Guar gum works similarly; some use it in frappés and to thicken hard cider.

**Failure mode (learn from it):** adding a teaspoon of dry xanthan straight into a
small (~20 oz) batch produced **white specks floating in the drink and stuck to
the evaporator** — "a disaster." Xanthan is only lightly water-soluble, so
whisk it into the liquid (or pre-blend with the dry sweetener) until fully
hydrated first.

**Slurpee vs. ICEE:** several users specifically want the dense, fine Slurpee
texture, not the airier ICEE. Xanthan + adequate sugar/allulose is the community's
answer; note the Slushi **cannot reproduce a truly carbonated ICEE** (carbonation
is always lost — see [community-faq-and-tips.md](community-faq-and-tips.md)).

## Sugar-free / diet sweeteners (the freezing part)

Ranked by how reliably the community reports success:

- **Allulose — the clear favorite.** Behaves like real sugar, gives the smoothest
  sugar-free result, freezes properly. Reported dosing clusters around
  **~1–1.5 Tbsp per 12 oz** (some use ~1 Tbsp per 8 oz). Dissolve first.
- **Splenda "Magic Baker" (an allulose-based blend)** — works well; some prefer
  its taste. Good for diet sodas.
- **Erythritol / monk-fruit-erythritol** — hit or miss; needs **more than the
  recipe-book amounts** and can be gritty. One working diet build: ~7 Tbsp
  erythritol + ¼ tsp salt + lemon juice to taste for a full batch. Poor results
  reported with Coke Zero specifically.
- **Food-grade glycerine/glycerol (~1%)** — experimental antifreeze; a few report
  it working for diet raspberry-type drinks on the lowest setting.
- **Plain stevia / aspartame / sucralose diet drinks alone — do NOT work.** They
  freeze rock-hard; the machine's low-sugar alert will fire. (Diet soda *does*
  work in a **spiked** recipe, where the alcohol is the antifreeze.)

## Putting it together — sugar-free Slurpee recipe pattern

1. Start with the sugar-free base (diet soda or water + sugar-free syrup).
2. Add a **real freezing agent**: allulose (~1 Tbsp/8–12 oz) or Splenda Magic
   Baker, fully dissolved.
3. Add a **pinch of xanthan gum (~¼ tsp)**, whisked in until hydrated — no dry
   specks.
4. Run the preset **1 bar warmer** than default if it trends icy.
5. If it still won't slush, it's under-sweetened, not under-thickened — add more
   allulose, not more xanthan.
